# Red Cross Emergency First-Aid Protocols
## Severe Bleeding (Hemorrhage)
Severe bleeding is a life-threatening emergency. The goal is to stop blood loss as quickly as possible.

1. Apply direct pressure to the wound using a clean cloth, gauze, or your hand if nothing else is available.
2. If bleeding continues, add more layers on top — do NOT remove soaked dressings.
3. If direct pressure fails and the wound is on a limb, apply a tourniquet 2-3 inches above the wound, never over a joint.
4. Note the time the tourniquet was applied. Do not loosen it.
5. Keep the victim warm and lying down. Elevate the legs if no spinal injury is suspected.
6. Call for emergency medical evacuation immediately.

## Fracture Immobilization
Fractures require stabilization to prevent further tissue damage and reduce pain.

1. Do NOT attempt to straighten a deformed limb.
2. Immobilize the joint above and below the fracture site.
3. Use splints made from rigid materials (boards, rolled magazines, tree branches padded with cloth).
4. Check circulation distal to the injury every 15 minutes.
5. Apply cold packs to reduce swelling, wrapped in cloth to prevent frostbite.

## Flood Response & Water Hazards
Rising water creates multiple simultaneous hazards: drowning, electrocution, contamination, and structural collapse.

1. Do NOT enter moving water deeper than ankle height.
2. Avoid contact with downed power lines — water conducts electricity.
3. Evacuate to highest available ground. Preferred targets: designated elevation points, reinforced buildings, or rooftops.
4. If trapped, signal for rescue using bright cloth, mirrors, or whistles.
5. After rescue, monitor for hypothermia even in warm climates — wet clothing accelerates heat loss.

## Multiple Casualty Triage (START Method)
When resources are limited, prioritize based on survivability.

- RED (Immediate): Respiratory distress after airway opening, uncontrolled bleeding, shock.
- YELLOW (Delayed): Fractures, burns <20% BSA, conscious but unable to walk.
- GREEN (Minor): Walking wounded, minor lacerations.
- BLACK (Deceased/Expectant): No respiration after airway opening, no pulse.

## Burn Management
1. Stop the burning process — remove from heat source.
2. Cool burns with clean water for 10-20 minutes. Do NOT use ice.
3. Cover with sterile non-stick dressing. Do NOT apply ointments to severe burns.
4. For burns >20% body surface area, initiate fluid resuscitation and evacuate immediately.
