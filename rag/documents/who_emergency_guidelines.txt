# WHO Emergency Health Guidelines
## Mass Casualty Incident (MCI) Response
In disasters, the standard of care shifts to doing the greatest good for the greatest number.

### Scene Safety and Command
- Establish incident command within 5 minutes of arrival.
- Identify and mark hazardous zones: Hot (danger), Warm (triage), Cold (treatment/transport).
- Maintain communication logs. In mesh-degraded environments, use runners or visual signals.

### Communicable Disease Control in Shelters
Overcrowding in evacuation centers increases risk of respiratory and diarrheal outbreaks.

1. Maintain 2-meter spacing between sleeping areas where possible.
2. Ensure handwashing stations with soap or alcohol rub at all entry/exit points.
3. Isolate individuals with fever, cough, or diarrhea in a separate ventilation zone.
4. Report clusters of illness to the epidemiology cell within 2 hours.

### Water, Sanitation, and Hygiene (WASH)
Safe water is the highest priority after immediate life threats.

- Minimum: 15 liters per person per day for drinking, cooking, and hygiene.
- If source is questionable: boil for 1 minute, or add chlorine tablets (0.2-0.5 mg/L residual after 30 min).
- Latrines: 1 per 50 people, located 30m from water sources, downhill.

### Mental Health First Aid in Disasters
Psychological distress is nearly universal in disaster survivors.

1. Listen without forcing discussion.
2. Provide accurate information about safety, resources, and next steps.
3. Maintain normal routines where possible.
4. Identify and refer individuals showing signs of acute stress disorder or dissociation.
5. Protect dignity — privacy is scarce in shelters but essential for recovery.

### Evacuation Priorities
When ordered to evacuate, the following order should be observed:
1. Hospitalized patients and those requiring life-support equipment.
2. Populations in flood plains, landslide zones, or structurally compromised buildings.
3. Special needs populations: elderly, disabled, pregnant women, infants.
4. General population in phased waves to prevent transport gridlock.
